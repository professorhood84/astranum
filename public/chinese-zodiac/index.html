Chinese Zodiac Guide

Chinese zodiac horoscopes, sign by sign

The Chinese zodiac assigns one of 12 animals to every birth year on a repeating cycle, then layers a five-element flavor on top — so no two "Dragon years," for example, feel quite the same. Find your animal below, read what it really means, and see who it naturally clashes and harmonizes with.

How your animal and element are determined

Your animal sign comes from your birth year on a repeating 12-year cycle: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig — then back to Rat. Your element comes from the last digit of your birth year and cycles every 10 years: digits 4–5 are Wood, 6–7 are Fire, 8–9 are Earth, 0–1 are Metal, and 2–3 are Water. Combined, the animal and element cycles create a full 60-year cycle before any exact year/animal/element combination repeats.

One important catch: the Chinese New Year doesn't fall on January 1 — it lands somewhere between January 21 and February 20 each Gregorian year. If you were born in that window, you may actually belong to the previous year's animal, not the one tied to your birth year number. Check the exact Chinese New Year date for your birth year before assuming your sign from the year alone.

  • San He (trine) — three animals, 4 years apart, that share natural harmony and instincts.
  • Liu He (paired harmony) — a secondary, one-to-one harmony pairing for each animal.
  • Liu Chong (clash) — the one animal directly opposite yours on the cycle, the classic "clash" pairing.
